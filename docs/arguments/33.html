Protective Service

Description

Protective service occupations are the people who protect people, property, and information from harm, crime, and emergencies. The group includes police officers, firefighters, security guards, correctional officers, and private investigators. Their work involves patrolling and monitoring, responding to incidents, enforcing rules and laws, restraining threats, and investigating suspected wrongdoing. Most public-sector roles require academy training and state certification, while many support roles require a high school diploma plus on-the-job instruction. Workers in this group keep communities safe and maintain public order.

Major group 33 of the U.S. O*NET-SOC register, containing 28 of the 1,016 professions listed on this site.

Workforce prevalence

Major group 33 accounts for 3,871,400 workers, or 2.27% of the U.S. workforce (2025). Employment and projected growth vary by occupation; see each profession’s page for its own prevalence and projected change.
