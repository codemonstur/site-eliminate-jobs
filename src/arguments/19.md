## Description

Life, physical, and social science occupations cover the people who study the natural world, living systems, and human society through observation and experiment. The group includes biologists, chemists, physicists, environmental scientists, psychologists, economists, and urban and regional planners. Typical duties involve designing studies, collecting and analyzing samples and data, interpreting results, and reporting findings to other scientists, policymakers, or the public. Most roles require a bachelor's degree, and research positions often call for a master's degree or Ph.D. Workers in this group produce the knowledge and evidence that drive scientific progress and informed decision-making.

Major group `19` of the U.S. O\*NET-SOC register, containing
**66** of the 1,016 professions listed on this site.

## Workforce prevalence

Major group `19` accounts for **1,628,500** workers, or **0.96%** of the U.S. workforce (2025). Employment and projected growth vary by occupation; see each profession's page for its own prevalence and projected change.

## Ways these jobs could be eliminated

### AI literature review and hypothesis generation

Models that scan research and propose new questions reduce the manual work of researchers. They can summarize papers and identify gaps.

This accelerates the research process and reduces the need for large research teams.

**Examples**

- [Elicit](https://elicit.com/)
- [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/)
- [Wikipedia: Literature review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literature_review)

### Robotic laboratories and automated experiments

Self-driving labs run experiments without human hands, reducing the need for lab technicians. They execute protocols and collect data automatically.

This compresses experimental work and reduces lab staffing.

**Examples**

- [Emerald Cloud Lab](https://www.emeraldcloudlab.com/)
- [Strateos](https://strateos.com/)
- [Wikipedia: Laboratory automation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratory_automation)

### Computational modeling and simulation

In-silico experiments replace physical trials, reducing the need for field researchers. Models simulate complex systems quickly.

This shifts research from physical to computational.

**Examples**

- [MATLAB](https://www.mathworks.com/)
- [COMSOL](https://www.comsol.com/)
- [Wikipedia: Computer simulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_simulation)

### AI-driven data collection and remote sensing

Automated gathering of field and satellite data reduces the need for field researchers. Sensors and satellites collect continuously.

This compresses data collection and reduces staffing.

**Examples**

- [Planet Labs](https://www.planet.com/)
- [Maxar](https://www.maxar.com/)
- [Wikipedia: Remote sensing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_sensing)

### Automated statistical analysis and reporting

Tools that analyze datasets and generate reports reduce the manual work of analysts. They run analyses and produce insights automatically.

This compresses analysis and reduces staffing.

**Examples**

- [R](https://www.r-project.org/)
- [Python](https://www.python.org/)
- [Wikipedia: Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics)
